# Consultant Boundary + Client Reality Notes

## Date

2026-05-13

## User Insight

The user previously operated as a business coach and repeatedly experienced business coaching turning into relationship counselling.

A common pattern:

- founder enthusiasm exceeds evidence
- partner is pulled into bookkeeping/admin
- stress transfers into the household
- adviser becomes mediator rather than business coach
- weak proof creates emotional and operational conflict

This is one reason the user wants Autopsy to remain a tool, not a high-touch coaching obligation.

## Product Implication

Autopsy should protect the founder, the partner, and the adviser by putting evidence in the centre before commitment.

The product should reduce emotional mediation by creating a shared diagnostic record.

## Consultant Market Implication

For consultants, Autopsy can become a structured diagnostic that:

- exposes weak evidence early
- separates intention from proof
- helps avoid bad-fit clients
- reduces emotional ambiguity
- gives both partners and adviser a common evidence base

## Boundary Doctrine

Autopsy should not create a dependency-heavy coaching model.

It should create evidence, permission control, and progression gates.

The future consultant product should give advisers a tool, not force the product owner into becoming the adviser.

## Strong Positioning Line

Before the business becomes a household problem, test the evidence together.

## Guardrail

Use this angle carefully.

The landing page should feel protective and reality-facing, not cynical or anti-founder.
