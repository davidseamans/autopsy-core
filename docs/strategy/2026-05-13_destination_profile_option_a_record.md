# Destination Profile — Option A Record

## Date

2026-05-13

## Decision Signal

Option A — Pre-Autopsy Intake — strongly resonated.

## Option A

Before the Autopsy diagnostic starts, ask the operator to declare what outcome the business must produce for the effort and risk to be worth it.

## Anchor Question

What outcome must this business produce for the effort and risk to be worth it?

## Why This Matters

If the user has no defined destination, the system cannot determine whether the business is progressing toward anything meaningful.

Guarantees are meaningless unless attached to a declared destination.

Examples:

- A guarantee of $1 million per year means nothing to someone who does not value that outcome.
- A guarantee of lifestyle freedom means nothing if the operator wants challenge, acquisition growth, or exit value.
- A guarantee of passive income means nothing if the operator needs meaning, leadership, or category excellence.

## User Experience Lesson

When desired success was not explicitly discovered from consulting clients, it often led to disaster because the adviser and client were operating from different definitions of success.

## Product Implication

Destination Profile may need to be introduced earlier than originally assumed.

It should be designed carefully so it does not feel intrusive, fluffy, or pressuring.

## Possible Destination Choices

- Survival income
- Replace employment
- Family security
- Lifestyle autonomy
- Passive ownership
- Growth and scale
- Acquisition improvement
- Exit value
- Challenge/excellence
- Industry leadership
- Not sure yet

## Guardrail

Do not turn Autopsy into life coaching.

This must remain operational:

- what outcome is required?
- what effort/risk is acceptable?
- what pathway does that imply?
- what proof must be produced?

## Architecture Fit

Autopsy = evidence of readiness
Destination Profile = reason for proceeding
Worksheet = proof required
Core/Sleeve = operating pathway
Guarantee = conditional contract attached to declared destination
